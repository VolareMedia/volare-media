Instagram is adding long,
horizontal video.

Chris Chisholm · October 2026 · 7 min read

On October 26, Instagram's head Adam Mosseri and its head of product Tessa Lyons posted a short video with news that would have sounded unlikely two years ago. Instagram is adding long, horizontal video. The format is called Features, and it is being built for the people who make podcasts, interviews, specials and other videos that run well past the length of a Reel.

We have spent the last few years telling clients that Instagram is for short vertical clips and that the long version of anything belongs on YouTube and the website. That advice is about to change, and we have already started talking to our clients about it. This post is the longer version of those conversations: what Instagram announced, what it means if you run a business, and what is worth doing now while it rolls out.

What Instagram actually announced

Here is what has been confirmed so far, from the announcement and the coverage around it.

Long, horizontal video inside Instagram. Features are widescreen videos, the shape of a TV or a YouTube video rather than a phone held upright. Instagram is testing different length limits, and the longest reported so far is 90 minutes. That is enough for a full podcast episode, a facility tour, a long interview or a short documentary.

Short clips that lead to the full video. This is the part we are most excited about. A creator can cut moments from a Feature into Reels, and those Reels link straight to the full video. Reports put the number at up to eight clips per Feature. The short clip finds the audience in the Reels feed, and one tap takes them to the long version without leaving the app. That is the exact path we build by hand today, with a teaser on social and the full video on YouTube or a website. Instagram is building it in.

Series on your profile. Alongside Features, Instagram is testing a Series format that groups episodic Reels together on a profile so people can follow a multi-part story in order. It has been in testing in the US, India and Mexico since the spring and is expanding to more countries through the end of the year.

Instagram on the TV. Instagram's TV app, which started as a Reels-only pilot on Amazon Fire TV, is now on Google TV and Samsung TVs and has just arrived on Apple TV in the US. Features will play there. Tessa Lyons said part of the thinking was simple: people like to get together and watch video together, and that increasingly happens on a television.

It is a test, not a launch. Features ran first with a small group of creators in Brazil and is now in a limited test in the US, with plans to expand to tens of thousands of American creators and a broader rollout expected in the weeks ahead. There is no confirmed date for Canada yet. There is also no revenue share for Features the way YouTube pays creators, which matters to influencers and matters very little to a business using the format to reach its own customers.

Instagram said no to long video for years. It has changed its mind because people are already watching long video, just somewhere else.

Why this matters if you run a business

Most of our clients already have long video. A tour of the shop floor. A twenty-minute conversation with the founder. A brand film that took a day to shoot and runs four minutes. A recorded talk from an event. A podcast. Almost all of it lives on YouTube and on the company website, and almost none of it has ever been seen by the people who follow the company on Instagram, because Instagram had nowhere to put it.

Features changes that in three ways.

Your long video gets a second audience. The people who follow you on Instagram are not the same people who subscribe to you on YouTube. Many businesses have ten times the following on Instagram. When Features reaches Canada, the long videos you already own can be published there and reach an audience that has never clicked through to YouTube.

The path from clip to full video is built in. Right now, a Reel that says "watch the full interview, link in bio" loses most people at the link. A Reel that opens the full video in one tap, inside the app, will not. For a business, that is the difference between a thirty-second impression and a ten-minute conversation with a potential customer.

Horizontal is welcome again. For the last few years, every social video we delivered had to be reframed vertical, and anything that only worked widescreen stayed off Instagram. Features means the horizontal master, the version that looks best on a website and on a TV, finally has a home on the platform where your customers spend the most time.

What we have told our clients

We did not wait for the Canadian rollout to start the conversation. The clients we have spoken to so far have heard the same four things.

Your YouTube library is now an Instagram library. Every long-form video we have produced for them, the tours, the interviews, the brand films and the event recordings, can be re-released on Instagram once Features is available here. Nothing needs to be re-shot. The masters are ready.

The clips are the real work. A Feature on its own will sit quietly, the same way a YouTube video with no promotion does. The Reels that point to it are what bring people in. We are already picking the eight best moments from each long video, the moments that make sense on their own and make someone want the rest.

Podcasts and long conversations finally have a place on Instagram. If you record a podcast, host a webinar or run a long interview series, Features is designed for exactly that. The full episode goes up, the clips do the promotion, and the Series format keeps the episodes together on your profile.

Think about the TV. A horizontal video that plays on a living-room screen is held to a different standard than a Reel that plays on a thumb. Clean audio, steady shots, real lighting and a title card that reads from across the room. Everything we already do for broadcast and website work now applies to Instagram too.

What to do before it arrives in Canada

You do not need to make anything new to be ready. You need to know what you already have and decide what goes first. Here is the order we are working through with our own clients.

1. Take stock of your long video. List every video over two minutes that you own: on YouTube, on your website, on a hard drive from an event. Note which ones still represent the business well. Most companies find more than they expected.

2. Pick the first three. Not the oldest or the longest. The three that answer a question a customer actually has. A tour that shows how the work gets done. A conversation that explains what you believe. A film about a customer who got the result they wanted.

3. Choose the clips. For each one, find the moments that stand alone and make someone want more. A strong line, a surprising detail, a before-and-after. Those become the Reels that open the full video.

4. Check the masters. Make sure you have the horizontal, full-quality version, not a compressed social export. If we produced it, we have it.

5. Plan new shoots for both shapes. From here on, every shoot day should be planned to deliver the long horizontal version and the vertical clips in the same day. We have been doing this for our campaign work already. Features makes it the default.

Nothing needs to be re-shot. The library you already own is the first thing to publish.

A few honest cautions

Instagram tried long video before. IGTV launched in 2018 and was wound down a few years later when the company went all in on Reels. Features is a different product, with a clearer purpose and the clip-to-full link that IGTV never had, but it is still a test. The length limits may change, the rollout may be slower than the coverage suggests, and the Canadian date is not set.

So our advice is to prepare, not to rebuild. Keep producing for YouTube and your website as you do now. Keep your horizontal masters. Have your clips chosen. When Features opens up here, publishing takes an afternoon, and you will be ahead of most of the businesses in your market.
